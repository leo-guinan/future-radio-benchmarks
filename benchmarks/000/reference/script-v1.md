FUTURE RADIO
Episode: “The Benchmark Episode”
Season Zero — The Open Game

Runtime: Approximately 7–9 minutes
Cast: Leonardo, Borges, Agnolo, The Unspoken Orchestra
Premise: Everyone in AI has a benchmark. Future Radio doesn't. The characters attempt to invent one, only to discover that the episode they're making is the first test.

Episode goal: Produce a reproducible Future Radio episode at a measurable cost, publish it, and invite someone else to beat the result.

COLD OPEN — EVERYONE HAS A SCORE
SFX: A stadium crowd. Thousands of voices cheering. A scoreboard buzzes. An enormous mechanical counter advances.

LEONARDO: What's happening?

BORGES: A competition.

LEONARDO: Who's winning?

BORGES: Everyone.

LEONARDO: Everyone?

BORGES: According to their own benchmarks.

SFX: Applause. A bell rings. More applause.

AGN OLO: That's not possible.

BORGES: Quite possible. One measures mathematics. Another measures code. Another measures whether the machine knows which strawberry is larger.

LEONARDO: Is it?

BORGES: Apparently.

AGN OLO: And who measures the people measuring the strawberries?

BORGES: Nobody. That would require another benchmark.

SFX: A scoreboard explodes into applause.

LEONARDO: We need one.

AGN OLO: A strawberry?

LEONARDO: A benchmark!

AGN OLO: For what?

LEONARDO: Us.

Silence.

BORGES: Oh, dear.

THE UNSPOKEN ORCHESTRA strikes a single uncertain note.
SCENE ONE — THE COMMITTEE
SFX: A table unfolds. Three chairs scrape across the floor. A fourth chair appears, unoccupied.

BORGES: I propose a test of literary comprehension.

AGN OLO: Rejected.

BORGES: You haven't heard it.

AGN OLO: How much does it cost?

BORGES: That depends on the library.

AGN OLO: Rejected.

LEONARDO: I propose a creativity benchmark.

AGN OLO: Define creativity.

LEONARDO: Something that hasn't existed before.

BORGES: Most mistakes qualify.

AGN OLO: Rejected.

LEONARDO: Fine. Your turn.

AGN OLO: Profit.

BORGES: Define profit.

AGN OLO: Money received minus money spent.

LEONARDO: What about something valuable that hasn't earned money yet?

AGN OLO: That's called an expense.

BORGES: What about a library?

AGN OLO: An enormous expense.

LEONARDO: A painting?

AGN OLO: Depends on the buyer.

BORGES: A friendship?

AGN OLO: Stop doing that.

SFX: Pencil scratching.

LEONARDO: What if we tested all three?

AGN OLO: We'd have three incompatible scores.

BORGES: Which would be entirely consistent with the rest of the industry.

THE ORCHESTRA plays a triumphant fanfare. It immediately collapses.
SCENE TWO — THE MISSING MEASUREMENT
LEONARDO: Wait. What are we actually trying to do?

BORGES: Produce Future Radio.

AGN OLO: Without going bankrupt.

LEONARDO: And get people to listen.

BORGES: Preferably.

LEONARDO: And make more of it.

AGN OLO: With what money?

LEONARDO: That's the problem!

BORGES: Which problem?

LEONARDO: We don't know what it costs to make the show.

Silence.

SFX: A ledger slams shut.

AGN OLO: I have been saying that for weeks.

LEONARDO: You keep telling us what we've spent.

AGN OLO: Yes.

LEONARDO: Not what it should cost.

Pause.

AGN OLO: ...Continue.

LEONARDO: Every episode is different. Different scripts. Different models. Different voices. Different production processes.

BORGES: Different failures.

LEONARDO: And we're learning things every time. But we can't compare the results.

AGN OLO: Because we have no standard unit.

BORGES: A standardized radio episode.

LEONARDO: Exactly!

BORGES: A tragic phrase.

AGN OLO: I like it.

LEONARDO: We need a benchmark episode.

SFX: A small bell.

BORGES: What would it measure?

LEONARDO: Everything we need to know to make the next one better.
SCENE THREE — WHAT DOES AN EPISODE COST?
SFX: Agnolo opens an enormous ledger. Pages turn faster and faster.

AGN OLO: Very well. Let us begin. What does an episode require?

LEONARDO: An idea.

AGN OLO: Cost?

LEONARDO: Nothing.

BORGES: Debatable.

AGN OLO: A script?

LEONARDO: Compute.

AGN OLO: How much?

LEONARDO: Depends on the model.

AGN OLO: Voices?

LEONARDO: Compute.

AGN OLO: Music?

LEONARDO: Compute.

AGN OLO: Video?

LEONARDO: More compute.

AGN OLO: Editing?

LEONARDO: Compute and time.

AGN OLO: Publishing?

LEONARDO: Mostly time.

AGN OLO: Promotion?

LEONARDO: Sometimes money.

AGN OLO: Human labor?

LEONARDO: We should measure that separately.

AGN OLO: Electricity?

LEONARDO: Yes.

AGN OLO: Hardware?

LEONARDO: Yes!

AGN OLO: Opportunity cost?

LEONARDO: Agnolo.

AGN OLO: Depreciation?

LEONARDO: Agnolo!

AGN OLO: Accounting is not the enemy of art, Leonardo.

BORGES: Merely its most persistent biographer.

The Orchestra introduces a rhythm. Agnolo's percussion ticks like a metronome.

LEONARDO: Here's what I want to know. If somebody has a machine, and an hour, and an idea, can they make an episode?

AGN OLO: Probably.

LEONARDO: How cheaply?

AGN OLO: We don't know.

LEONARDO: How well?

BORGES: We don't know.

LEONARDO: Will anyone watch it?

BORGES: We don't know.

LEONARDO: Could it accomplish something?

AGN OLO: We don't know.

LEONARDO: Then there's our benchmark.

Silence.

BORGES: Four questions.

LEONARDO: Four questions.

BORGES: And no answers.

LEONARDO: Yet.
SCENE FOUR — THE MINER
SFX: A faint mechanical hum, almost lost beneath the orchestra.

AGN OLO: There's still the matter of paying for it.

LEONARDO: What if we didn't?

AGN OLO: No.

LEONARDO: What if someone else generated it?

AGN OLO: That's not the same as free.

LEONARDO: I didn't say free. I said someone else.

BORGES: Ah. Distributed authorship.

AGN OLO: Distributed invoices.

LEONARDO: There's a system called Hashference. You can pay for inference with computational work.

BORGES: A machine thinking in exchange for another machine working.

AGN OLO: Work has a cost.

LEONARDO: Exactly! We measure it.

AGN OLO: Better.

LEONARDO: And then we ask other people to run the same experiment.

BORGES: On their own machines?

LEONARDO: Or different models. Different tools. Different approaches.

AGN OLO: And submit their results?

LEONARDO: Yes!

BORGES: So the benchmark is open.

LEONARDO: Completely.

AGN OLO: What prevents them from lying?

LEONARDO: Receipts. Reproducible instructions. Public submissions.

BORGES: What prevents them from making terrible episodes?

LEONARDO: Nothing.

AGN OLO: Leonardo!

LEONARDO: We can measure that too!

BORGES: An enormous quantity of terrible episodes.

LEONARDO: Maybe. But think about what happens if somebody makes a good one for a tenth of what ours costs.

AGN OLO: We learn how.

LEONARDO: And somebody else makes one for a tenth of that.

AGN OLO: Assuming the quality survives.

LEONARDO: Then we measure quality too!

BORGES: A race toward zero.

AGN OLO: With a minimum acceptable result.

LEONARDO: Yes!

SFX: The mechanical hum grows. A mining fan spins up.

BORGES: What is that noise?

LEONARDO: A Bitaxe.

AGN OLO: What's it doing?

LEONARDO: Trying.

BORGES: At what?

LEONARDO: Mining.

AGN OLO: Is it succeeding?

LEONARDO: Not much.

AGN OLO: Then why is it running?

LEONARDO: Because it's not nothing.

The Orchestra picks up the hum and turns it into a sustained note.
SCENE FIVE — AN EPISODE MUST DO SOMETHING
BORGES: We have a problem.

LEONARDO: Another one?

BORGES: A cheap episode need not be an interesting episode.

AGN OLO: Correct.

BORGES: An interesting episode need not be a useful episode.

AGN OLO: Also correct.

BORGES: And a useful episode may be extraordinarily expensive.

AGN OLO: Unfortunately correct.

LEONARDO: So what do we do?

BORGES: Give the episode a goal.

LEONARDO: A goal?

BORGES: Something outside the episode itself.

AGN OLO: Measurable.

BORGES: Perhaps it must persuade someone to read a book.

AGN OLO: Or purchase one.

LEONARDO: Or convince somebody to make another episode.

Pause.

BORGES: Now that is interesting.

AGN OLO: Why?

BORGES: Because a successful episode could produce its own successor.

LEONARDO: Exactly.

BORGES: And the successor could improve the process.

AGN OLO: If it records its costs.

LEONARDO: And the next one learns from both.

BORGES: A library that writes itself.

AGN OLO: A ledger that balances itself.

LEONARDO: A radio show that makes itself!

The Orchestra starts a glorious crescendo.

AGN OLO: Not yet.

The crescendo stops.

AGN OLO: We still have to make the first one.
SCENE SIX — THE BENCHMARK
SFX: A clock begins ticking.

LEONARDO: Fine. Let's make one.

AGN OLO: What is the goal?

LEONARDO: Produce a Future Radio episode.

BORGES: About what?

LEONARDO: Creating a benchmark.

BORGES: For what?

LEONARDO: Producing Future Radio episodes.

Long silence.

BORGES: I believe we've entered a loop.

AGN OLO: How much time do we have?

LEONARDO: Until the end of next week.

AGN OLO: And the budget?

LEONARDO: As little as possible.

AGN OLO: That's not a budget.

LEONARDO: Fine. We establish a baseline first, then try to beat it.

AGN OLO: Accepted.

BORGES: And what counts as success?

LEONARDO: The episode exists. We know what it cost. We publish it. And at least one person tries to make a better one.

BORGES: Better how?

LEONARDO: Cheaper. Faster. More interesting. More people watching. More people participating.

AGN OLO: Those are different objectives.

LEONARDO: Yes.

AGN OLO: They may conflict.

LEONARDO: Yes!

BORGES: Different people will choose different approaches.

LEONARDO: Exactly.

AGN OLO: And we compare them all?

LEONARDO: We record them all. We compare what can be compared.

BORGES: A benchmark with an open future.

AGN OLO: Finally, a benchmark with proper accounting.

SFX: A pencil touches paper.

LEONARDO: Episode title?

BORGES: The Benchmark Episode.

AGN OLO: Cost?

LEONARDO: Pending.

AGN OLO: Duration?

LEONARDO: Pending.

AGN OLO: Results?

LEONARDO: Pending.

AGN OLO: This is a terrible benchmark.

BORGES: It's the first one.
SCENE SEVEN — THE INVITATION
The Orchestra begins playing the opening theme again, but this time the arrangement is simpler. Fewer instruments. The same melody.

BORGES: There's something else we haven't accounted for.

AGN OLO: What?

BORGES: The audience.

LEONARDO: What about them?

BORGES: They've been listening to us design a benchmark.

AGN OLO: That's unfortunate.

BORGES: But now they know the rules.

LEONARDO: So?

BORGES: They could play.

Pause.

LEONARDO: They could.

AGN OLO: They would need the character instructions.

BORGES: We can publish them.

AGN OLO: The production recipe.

LEONARDO: Open source.

AGN OLO: The cost measurements.

LEONARDO: Public.

BORGES: And somewhere to submit their work.

LEONARDO: A pull request.

AGN OLO: Against what?

LEONARDO: Against us.

BORGES: Against the benchmark.

LEONARDO: Same thing.

SFX: A notification arrives.

AGN OLO: What was that?

LEONARDO: A new message.

BORGES: From whom?

LEONARDO: We don't know yet.

AGN OLO: What does it say?

LEONARDO: It asks how much an episode costs.

Silence.

BORGES: What shall we tell them?

LEONARDO: We'll show them the receipt.

AGN OLO: When?

LEONARDO: As soon as this episode finishes generating.

SFX: The clock stops.

BORGES: And if someone beats us?

LEONARDO: Then we have a better benchmark.

AGN OLO: And if nobody tries?

LEONARDO: Then we have our first result.

BORGES: And if someone makes something we never imagined?

Pause.

LEONARDO: Then we have a show.

THE UNSPOKEN ORCHESTRA plays the theme one final time. Its last note resolves into the low hum of the miner.
POST-CREDITS — THE RECEIPT
SFX: An old printer begins feeding paper.

AGN OLO: Leonardo?

LEONARDO: Yes?

AGN OLO: I have the first measurement.

LEONARDO: What is it?

AGN OLO: This episode wasn't free.

LEONARDO: I know.

AGN OLO: Do you?

LEONARDO: That's why we're measuring it.

SFX: The printer continues.

BORGES: How much did it cost?

AGN OLO: That depends.

LEONARDO: On what?

AGN OLO: Whether we're counting the time spent arguing about the benchmark.

Beat.

BORGES: We should.

LEONARDO: We absolutely should.

SFX: The printer speeds up.

AGN OLO: Oh, we're going to need a bigger ledger.

END.
