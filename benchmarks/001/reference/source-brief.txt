# Future Radio: The Computational Treadmill

**Subtitle:** The audience becomes the power source.

**Genre:** Comedy, technological discovery, economic experiment.

**Characters:** Leonardo, Borges, Agnolo, Eval, the Unspoken Orchestra.

**Premise:** Future Radio needs energy to produce more radio. Leonardo discovers that listeners own computers that can perform useful work while they listen. He decides to turn the audience into a distributed production-support system.

**Episode goal:** Demonstrate a real browser session that lets a viewer voluntarily contribute verifiable computational work while listening.

**Episode clock:** 60 minutes for the in-world demonstration, with smaller sub-games.

## Cold open — The audience is doing nothing

*Sound: The orchestra is playing beautifully. A ticking clock enters the arrangement.*

Leonardo is celebrating. Future Radio has listeners.

Agnolo asks what the listeners are producing.

Leonardo says they're listening.

Agnolo asks how much electricity is being consumed by all the computers playing the broadcast.

Leonardo realizes something: the audience is already spending energy to receive the show, but very little of that computation contributes to making the next episode.

Borges points out that listening is not doing nothing. The value of the audience's attention is distinct from the computational power of their devices.

Leonardo has an idea.

"What if they could help power the show while watching it?"

Agnolo asks the obvious question.

"With whose electricity?"

*The orchestra stops.*

## Act I — The machine that watches itself

**Goal:** Find a way for an ordinary viewer to contribute computational work without opening an account or paying money.

Leonardo proposes using the viewer's browser.

Borges discovers Quai's x402 Work protocol: a website can request a small proof-of-work computation before granting access to a resource.

The characters explain the mechanism:

1. A browser requests a resource.
2. The server requests proof of work.
3. The browser performs a bounded mining computation.
4. The server verifies the result.
5. The resource becomes available.

Agnolo is impressed that the mechanism doesn't require the viewer to purchase cryptocurrency.

But he identifies a problem.

"One computation unlocks one request. You want the computer to keep helping for an entire episode."

Leonardo launches a sub-game.

**Sub-game 1: Can a browser continuously accept and complete small mining jobs while an episode plays?**

Clock: 10 minutes.

## Act II — The treadmill

**Goal:** Make computational participation compatible with a pleasant listening experience.

The first experiment works, but Leonardo tries to run as much computation as possible.

The audio stutters. The fan spins up. Agnolo starts measuring the electricity.

Borges reminds him that computation isn't free just because Future Radio isn't paying for it.

Eval asks whether the audience agreed to contribute.

Leonardo redesigns the experience.

Three choices:

- Listen normally, with no mining.
- Contribute a small, adjustable amount of computation.
- Enter Focus Mode: a timed, distraction-minimized session where computation pauses when the viewer leaves.

Mining must always be optional and interruptible.

The characters discover something interesting: they can measure computational participation, but they cannot infer mental attention from it.

**Sub-game 2: Can we maintain uninterrupted playback while running voluntary, bounded computation?**

Clock: 10 minutes.

The Unspoken Orchestra begins using the rhythm of verified work as percussion, so the contribution becomes audible without revealing private listener information.

## Act III — Does it pay?

**Goal:** Determine whether the work has economic value.

Leonardo announces that Future Radio is now powered by its audience.

Agnolo interrupts.

"How much money did we make?"

Leonardo points to a large number of hashes.

Agnolo points to an empty revenue column.

Borges explains that the x402 jobs represent real mining work, but a verified payment receipt does not guarantee that a block reward was earned.

Eval distinguishes three measurements:

- Verified work performed.
- Actual rewards received.
- Cost of producing the episode.

The characters realize that contribution, attention, and revenue are three different things.

**Sub-game 3: Produce one validated contribution record and reconcile it against actual production costs and any mining rewards.**

Clock: 15 minutes.

The result must come from the real system. If the experiment earns nothing, the characters report zero.

## Act IV — The audience joins the game

The viewer interface comes online.

Leonardo invites the audience to participate.

The screen shows an episode, a voluntary mining control, and a public aggregate contribution meter.

Each viewer can choose how much computational effort to contribute.

The characters introduce their next experiment: can audience-supported work offset the cost of generating one minute of Future Radio?

Borges proposes preserving every experiment so the next producer doesn't have to rediscover the same techniques.

Agnolo starts the ledger.

Eval insists on a control group that watches without mining.

## Ending — The treadmill goes nowhere

Leonardo watches the meter move.

He says, "We're moving."

Agnolo replies, "We're sitting still."

Borges says, "That's what treadmills do."

Leonardo asks, "Then where are we going?"

Borges answers, "We'll know when we see what the work produces."

The orchestra begins its next composition.

A new game appears:

**Can the audience generate enough useful work to support the production of the next episode?**

The clock starts.

*End of episode. The real experiment continues.*

---

## Production requirements

- Use the existing Future Radio character identities, voices, and sound conventions.
- The comedic tension comes from Leonardo's ambition, Agnolo's accounting, Borges's evidence, and Eval's skepticism.
- No character promises mining revenue or claims that computation verifies human attention.
- Any presented technical results must be taken from real experiment logs.
- The episode can be released with a demonstration-in-progress ending if the prototype is not yet complete.
- Use the Unspoken Orchestra to communicate the relationship between energy, time, and uncertainty.

**Desired final artifact:** A publishable audio episode with an accompanying playable experiment page and a link to the public results ledger.
