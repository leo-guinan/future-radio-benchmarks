# FUTURE RADIO — THE COMPUTATIONAL TREADMILL
Season Zero — The Open Game
Adapted from the supplied treatment. Demonstration-in-progress edition.

## COLD OPEN — THE AUDIENCE IS DOING NOTHING
MUSIC: An original Orchestra theme, with a small ticking clock.
LEONARDO: We have listeners!
AGNOLO: What are they producing?
LEONARDO: They're listening.
AGNOLO: And their computers?
LEONARDO: Also listening. Less attentively.
BORGES: Listening is not doing nothing. An audience gives us something a computer cannot measure by counting its instructions.
AGNOLO: I was counting its electricity.
LEONARDO: Wait. Their machines are already working to receive the show. What if a little more of that work could help make the next one?
BORGES: A listener is not a power socket.
LEONARDO: A voluntary power socket.
AGNOLO: With whose electricity?
PAUSE: 1.2
MUSIC: The Orchestra stops on an uncertain note.
EVAL: We appear to have discovered consent before discovering the product.
LEONARDO: Good. We can put it near the beginning.

## ACT ONE — THE MACHINE THAT WATCHES ITSELF
SFX: A pencil draws a small browser window.
LEONARDO: An ordinary browser. No new account. No purchase of cryptocurrency. Could it do a small job while the radio plays?
BORGES: There is a protocol called x four oh two Work. A server asks for a proof of computation before granting access to a resource.
AGNOLO: A toll booth that accepts perspiration.
BORGES: Computational perspiration. Request a resource, receive a work offer, solve it, send the proof, and let the server verify it.
LEONARDO: Then the audience can keep helping for an entire episode!
EVAL: That last sentence contains a new experiment. One proof pays for one request. Continuous participation needs its own controls.
AGNOLO: And a limit.
LEONARDO: A one-hour game. Smaller jobs inside it. A smaller clock inside each job.
BORGES: Clocks all the way down.
EVAL: First sub-game: can we accept bounded jobs while playback continues? Ten minutes is the proposed test window, not a result we have already measured.
LEONARDO: The prototype is on the desk.
AGNOLO: Put the receipt beside it.

## ACT TWO — THE TREADMILL
SFX: A brief, obviously theatrical fan sound.
LEONARDO: First design: use all the computation!
EVAL: Rejected before testing.
LEONARDO: You didn't even let the fan finish.
BORGES: A sound effect is not evidence of an overheated listener.
AGNOLO: Nor is somebody else's electricity a free production budget.
LEONARDO: Fine. Three choices. Listen normally. Contribute a little work. Or enter Focus Mode.
EVAL: Listening normally must start no computation. Contributions need a separate, explicit choice.
BORGES: Focus Mode may reduce distractions. It cannot certify what a person is thinking.
LEONARDO: What if we can prove the computer stayed on the page?
EVAL: Then you know something about the computer's page. You have not measured attention.
AGNOLO: The audience has escaped your spreadsheet.
LEONARDO: One background worker. A three-second job limit. A hundred thousand guesses at most. Rest between jobs. Stop means stop.
EVAL: Pause the audio or hide the page, and work stops. A failed job stops the loop. It does not quietly start another attempt.
BORGES: The right to listen includes the right to contribute nothing.
MUSIC: The theme returns with fewer instruments and more space.

## ACT THREE — THE ADDRESS ON THE TREADMILL
LEONARDO: So Future Radio is powered by its audience!
AGNOLO: Show me the beneficiary.
LEONARDO: The what?
AGNOLO: The address that receives any potential reward.
BORGES: The public Quai demonstration has its own configured recipient. That is not an established Future Radio payout address.
PAUSE: 1.2
LEONARDO: So a successful demonstration could demonstrate the wrong business model.
EVAL: It can demonstrate real access payment. It cannot demonstrate that the show received money.
AGNOLO: Your treadmill is plugged into somebody else's ledger.
LEONARDO: Then we label it.
BORGES: Official demonstration work. Beneficiary disclosed before starting. Future Radio funding branch not configured.
EVAL: And the demonstration distinguishes live-source receipts from practice receipts. Practice must not become network work through enthusiastic typography.
LEONARDO: We can still make the page playable.
AGNOLO: Yes. We can demonstrate the mechanism while admitting that the financing experiment is unfinished.
LEONARDO: A demonstration in progress.
BORGES: An honest title for a door that is not yet open.

## ACT FOUR — DOES IT PAY?
SFX: A ledger opens. Three quiet taps.
AGNOLO: How much money did we make?
LEONARDO: I was going to show you a large number of hashes.
AGNOLO: I have prepared an empty revenue column especially for that answer.
EVAL: Three records. Work accepted by a verifier. Actual rewards received. Cost of producing this episode.
BORGES: An access receipt proves that a proof was accepted for a request. It does not establish a block reward.
LEONARDO: What does our ledger say?
EVAL: It reports accepted receipts by source. It does not turn practice into live work, or a moving meter into an invoice paid.
AGNOLO: And the show's reward records?
BORGES: No configured show payout address. No reconciled network-reward claim. Those are unfinished measurements, not a secret profit.
LEONARDO: What about our costs?
AGNOLO: Voice synthesis credits. The production recipe. Compute time where recorded. Unknown dollar charges, human effort and energy stay visible.
BORGES: A blank field is more useful than an invented zero.
EVAL: And zero accepted receipts is a valid result if the real system has accepted none.
LEONARDO: The ledger can disappoint us?
AGNOLO: That is its most important feature.

## ACT FIVE — THE AUDIENCE JOINS THE GAME
MUSIC: The Orchestra adds a small, irregular percussion part.
LEONARDO: The page has an episode, a voluntary work control, and a results ledger.
EVAL: No work starts merely because the page opened or the audio began. Read the beneficiary and bounds before choosing to start.
BORGES: If a receipt arrives, the Orchestra can play a quiet tick. An accepted contribution becomes audible without turning a listener into a public profile.
AGNOLO: And when no receipt arrives?
LEONARDO: The Orchestra rests.
BORGES: A surprisingly economical instrument.
EVAL: Second sub-game: compare playback with computation off and on. Record stalls and the length of the actual test. Do not claim an uninterrupted hour from a short rehearsal.
LEONARDO: And the third?
AGNOLO: Reconcile one validated record against production costs and genuine rewards. Fifteen minutes is the proposed window. It may end with missing evidence.
BORGES: Preserve the experiment so the next producer can change a step without rediscovering the whole machine.
LEONARDO: Could audience work offset the cost of one minute of Future Radio?
EVAL: That is our next question. It is not today's answer.

## ENDING — THE TREADMILL GOES NOWHERE
SFX: A pencil places three empty columns on paper.
LEONARDO: We're moving.
AGNOLO: We're sitting still.
BORGES: That's what treadmills do.
LEONARDO: Then where are we going?
BORGES: We'll know when we see what the work produces.
EVAL: Until then: contribution is not attention. A receipt is not revenue. And someone else must still choose whether to participate.
LEONARDO: Can the audience help support the next episode?
AGNOLO: Give me a real result, a real cost, and the correct address. Then we can ask how far the belt has taken us.
BORGES: The experiment remains open.
LEONARDO: And the listening remains free of the experiment.
MUSIC: The original theme resolves into a clock tick, then silence.
