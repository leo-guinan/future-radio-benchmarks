# Future Radio — Visual Grammar v0.1
### A world constructed from within

**Foundational principle**

A frame is a world with boundaries. Any surface inside that world can contain another frame. A character who can manipulate that surface can change what exists within it.

A completed construction can be promoted from its containing frame into the surrounding world.

The history of that construction remains part of the artifact.

## 1. The seven operations

Every visual transformation should be expressible through these operations:

| Operation | Meaning | Visual expression |
|---|---|---|
| **INSCRIBE** | Create new information | Lines, marks, symbols, objects appear |
| **ALTER** | Modify existing information | Move, erase, reshape, recolor |
| **ANIMATE** | Give something behavior through time | Blink, walk, vibrate, react |
| **FRAME** | Establish a world within another | Page, monitor, window, photograph |
| **PROMOTE** | Move an object into its containing world | A drawing emerges from paper |
| **PROPAGATE** | Let a change affect surrounding objects | Drawn light illuminates a room |
| **COMPOSE** | Combine constructions into something larger | Individual objects become a scene |

These operations can be nested. A drawing can contain a character drawing another drawing.

## 2. Five states of existence

Every constructed object can pass through five states.

**I. Representation**

An object exists only as information on a surface. A microphone is drawn on a page.

**II. Construction**

A character visibly modifies the representation. New strokes appear, disappear, or change.

**III. Agency**

The represented object begins exhibiting behavior. The microphone vibrates or produces sound.

**IV. Emergence**

The object interacts with something outside its containing frame. Its shadow crosses the paper's edge. Its sound reaches the room.

**V. Integration**

The object becomes an independently persistent part of the surrounding world. Other characters can interact with it. The original representation may remain as a record.

Not every object needs to reach integration. A drawing can remain a drawing indefinitely.

## 3. Every character has capabilities

Characters have verbs that describe how they can change the world.

**Leonardo — Create**

Draw, invent, animate, transform, compose.

He can make previously nonexistent forms visible and give them behavior.

**Borges — Remember**

Find, reveal, retrieve, connect, preserve.

He can bring information from records into the scene, giving it a visible form.

**Agnolo — Account**

Count, allocate, constrain, compare, reconcile.

He can make the costs and consequences of actions visible and alter what resources are available.

**Eval — Test**

Duplicate, vary, measure, select, reject.

He can create competing versions of a scene, compare their outcomes, and preserve the results.

New capabilities can be learned and demonstrated inside the story. A character's abilities are not limited to the initial vocabulary.

## 4. The rules of construction

**Rule A — Locality**

A character's operation initially affects only the targeted object or subframe. Changes propagate outward only when an interaction causes them.

**Rule B — Continuity**

An object retains its identity across transformations. Its position, proportions, appearance, and history cannot change accidentally.

**Rule C — Visible causality**

The audience should be able to follow what caused a meaningful change. Characters do not need to explain it.

**Rule D — Persistent consequences**

When an object is constructed, it remains available to the world until something alters or removes it.

**Rule E — Recursive composition**

Any constructed surface can host another construction. The same rules apply at every level.

**Rule F — Reversibility where possible**

A construction can be replayed, rewound, revised, or branched. Irreversible story events can still be preserved as immutable versions.

**Rule G — Escalation through demonstrated capability**

Start with simple, observable changes. More complex constructions should emerge from operations the audience has already seen.

## 5. The grammar of crossing a boundary

A construction becomes part of its parent world through a recognizable sequence:

1. **Contact:** The constructed object reaches its frame's boundary.
2. **Evidence:** Something crosses that boundary — sound, shadow, light, movement, or matter.
3. **Transition:** The containing surface loses its exclusive control over the object.
4. **Reparenting:** The object acquires a persistent position and identity in the larger world.
5. **Consequence:** The larger world responds to its presence.

A boundary breach does not require a camera cut.

The camera can remain fixed as a flat drawing becomes a three-dimensional object occupying the same space.

## 6. The camera

The camera reveals changes in the structure of the world.

- **Hold** while construction occurs.
- **Push in** when entering a subframe.
- **Pull back** when revealing a larger containing world.
- **Track** an object crossing a boundary.
- **Rotate** when revealing a newly constructed dimension.
- **Cut** when continuity itself has narrative value, not merely to conceal difficult animation.

Favor transformations within a continuous shot for moments of discovery.

## 7. The visual and sound vocabulary

The initial visual style is restrained:

- Warm paper, pencil graphite, charcoal outlines.
- Small accents of copper and amber for activation.
- Visible construction marks and occasional imperfect strokes.
- Depth emerges through shadows, parallax, and perspective.
- Finished objects preserve visual evidence of their drawn origins.

Sound should reveal the type of operation occurring. Pencil friction becomes static; a moving waveform becomes voice; the emergence of an object gives it physical resonance.

Silence is an important state: the world is initially motionless until something changes.

## 8. The executable scene model

Every capability invocation should have a record:

- **Actor:** Who performs the operation?
- **Target:** What object or subframe changes?
- **Operation:** Which construction verb applies?
- **Inputs:** What information or existing objects are required?
- **Timeline:** When does it begin, and how does it progress?
- **Output:** What persistent object or behavior results?
- **Scope:** Where is the change allowed to occur?
- **Provenance:** What generated the construction, and who contributed?
- **Cost:** What resources were consumed?

An animation frame is a rendered view of the current world state, not a fresh generation of the whole world.

## 9. The audience contract

The audience should learn the rules by watching characters discover and use them.

The characters do not need to know they are in a movie. They use the capabilities available in their world to pursue ordinary goals.

The viewer understands the magic because the construction is visible.

**The final doctrine:**

*Creation is an action. Animation is evidence of action. The world is the accumulated consequence of those actions.*
