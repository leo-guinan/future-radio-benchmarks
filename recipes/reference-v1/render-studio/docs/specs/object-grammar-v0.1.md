# Future Radio — Object Grammar v0.1
## Construction, Correspondence, and Communication

### Foundational principle

Objects in Future Radio are not merely visual props. They may contain executable capabilities that change the world.

Every instrument has:

- A **domain**: what part of reality it can affect.
- An **interface**: how a character gives it instructions.
- An **agency level**: how independently it can act.
- A **boundary rule**: which frames its effects may cross.
- A **persistence rule**: what remains after its operation finishes.
- A **cost**: what resources its operation consumes.

An object can be constructed, commanded, modified, reused, and passed to another character.

## 1. The Pencil — Construction

**Doctrine: The pencil makes things exist.**

The pencil operates on Leonardo's constructed world.

**Domain:** Local world state, drawable surfaces, objects, actors, and frames.

**Control:** Direct manipulation. Leonardo guides its movements.

**Capabilities:**
- INSCRIBE — Create persistent visual objects.
- ALTER — Change existing objects.
- FRAME — Construct nested worlds.
- ANIMATE — Give constructed objects behavior.
- COMPOSE — Combine objects into larger constructions.

**Boundary:** Changes begin locally and only cross frame boundaries through permitted operations.

**Visual identity:** Graphite, sketch lines, pressure, imperfect marks, visible construction.

**Sound identity:** Dry friction, paper texture, distinct pencil contact and lift sounds.

**Persistence:** The construction survives when the pencil stops moving.

**Narrative meaning:** "I can make something."

The pencil is not inherently autonomous. Its physical motion represents Leonardo's actions.

## 2. The Quill — Correspondence

**Doctrine: The quill gives intention a form that can travel.**

The quill is an enchanted instrument that can interpret a character's request and produce a written artifact.

**Domain:** Language, correspondence, invitations, petitions, and messages intended for recipients beyond the local frame.

**Control:** Command and review. Leonardo provides the intention, recipient, constraints, and authority to proceed.

**Capabilities:**
- LISTEN — Receive the intended message.
- DRAFT — Propose written language.
- INSCRIBE — Render words as calligraphic strokes.
- REVISE — Modify a draft in response to instruction.
- PRESENT — Display a completed letter for review.
- PREPARE — Address, fold, seal, or package a letter.
- DISPATCH — Initiate an authorized delivery through an available channel.

These are high-level capabilities. They may compile into existing scene-log operations.

**Boundary:** The quill can prepare an artifact intended to leave the scene, but cannot independently establish a real-world communication channel or send externally without authorization.

**Visual identity:** Floating feather, articulated nib, graceful motion, ink with variable stroke width, occasional decorative flourishes.

**Sound identity:** Quiet nib contact, textured ink-on-paper friction, tiny ink and paper sounds, subtle resonance when summoned.

**Persistence:** A letter remains a readable, editable, attributable artifact after the quill stops writing.

**Narrative meaning:** "I can ask someone."

The quill has delegated agency. It may choose lettering, phrasing, and composition within its instructions, but Leonardo retains responsibility for the message.

## 3. The Radio — Communication

**Doctrine: The radio connects voices across frames and times.**

The radio receives and transmits signals. It does not necessarily know when or where their sources exist.

**Domain:** Audio signals, communication, broadcasts, listening, and correspondence through time.

**Control:** Tuning, routing, transmission, and reception.

**Capabilities:**
- TUNE — Select a signal or temporal direction.
- RECEIVE — Make an incoming observation audible.
- BROADCAST — Produce an outgoing signal.
- ROUTE — Connect signals between frames or characters.
- RECORD — Preserve a transmission.
- REPLAY — Reproduce a prior recording or observation.

**Boundary:** Signals may cross spatial, temporal, and nested-frame boundaries according to the world's timespace rules.

**Visual identity:** Waveforms, moving dials, luminous indicators, vibrating antennae.

**Sound identity:** Static, tuning tones, voice, music, feedback, and signal transitions.

**Persistence:** A signal may remain available as a recording after the original transmission ends.

**Narrative meaning:** "Someone can answer."

## 4. Three levels of agency

**Tool — Direct action**

The user controls the object's physical operation.

Example: Leonardo uses the pencil to draw a table.

**Instrument — Delegated action**

The user specifies an intention. The instrument executes within constraints.

Example: Leonardo commands the quill to write a sponsorship request.

**Agent — Independent continuation**

The object or character can pursue an assigned goal across multiple actions, within explicit permissions.

Example: A future quill might prepare multiple drafts, compare them, and recommend one for Leonardo's approval.

The quill begins as an instrument, not an unrestricted agent.

## 5. Common object lifecycle

Every executable object may pass through:

1. CONSTRUCTED — The object has a persistent representation.
2. ACTIVATED — Its supported behaviors are available.
3. COMMANDED — A character gives it a valid instruction.
4. EXECUTING — It performs actions over time.
5. REVIEWED — A character inspects any artifact requiring approval.
6. COMPLETED — The operation finishes and its consequences persist.

External correspondence adds separate states: DRAFTED, APPROVED, DISPATCHED, DELIVERED, and REPLIED.

These states must never be conflated. Finishing a letter does not mean it has been sent, and sending it does not mean it has been received or answered.

## 6. The quill's command contract

A command must identify:

- actor_id — Who is giving the instruction?
- instrument_id — Which quill receives it?
- recipient — Who is being addressed?
- intent — What does the character hope to accomplish?
- context — What facts are available?
- constraints — Tone, length, style, factual restrictions.
- output_frame — Where the writing should appear.
- approval_required — Whether a review is required.

The quill converts the command into two connected outputs:

**Semantic artifact:** The actual readable letter text.

**Visual artifact:** A sequence of glyph strokes, layout instructions, nib movements, and ink properties.

A change to the semantic artifact must trigger a corresponding change to the visual artifact.

The scene log records which version of the letter was written.

## 7. Causality and time

Construction sounds should normally follow the physical action producing them.

Correspondence signals can follow the more flexible timespace rules already established.

A letter might be visible before its words are completed. A response might be heard before a letter is sent. These are allowed narrative effects, but the event log must distinguish an observation from an actual delivery or response.

The renderer must not interpret fictional signal arrival as proof that real-world correspondence occurred.

## 8. The central relationship

The pencil creates the quill.

The quill creates the letter.

The letter carries a request beyond the studio.

A reply may return through the radio.

That reply may give Leonardo the resources, knowledge, or opportunity to create something else.

**Construction → Correspondence → Communication → New construction.**

This cycle is the foundational visual metaphor for Future Radio and its connection to story.markets.

A character can make an artwork that asks for the support needed to make the next artwork.

**Final doctrine:**

*The pencil changes what exists.*

*The quill changes who can be reached.*

*The radio changes what can be heard.*
