Part II — What that looks like
The visual progression of the first scene should be:









1. The drawing

Leonardo exists as pencil marks on paper.










2. The construction

Leonardo begins drawing the world around himself.










3. The emergence

The drawing becomes a room, then the room becomes alive.


Conceptual visual references for the sequence, not final character designs.
The important cinematic decision is that the page doesn't disappear. The page becomes the first piece of architecture.

Its edges become the floor. Its pencil lines become furniture. Its shadows become depth. Leonardo is not transported somewhere else; he constructs somewhere to be.
Part III — The first scene
Future Radio — "The First Line"
Cold open · 90 seconds

FADE IN.
00:00–00:12 — The picture
Black.

A thin scratching sound.

A single graphite line appears against warm, ivory paper.

Then another.

The camera slowly reveals a drawing of Leonardo: a little tin robot, sitting at a desk, holding a pencil.

He's unfinished. One of his hands is only an outline. The desk ends halfway across the page. Around him is nothing but blank paper.

Silence.

A tiny movement.

The pencil in his illustrated hand twitches.

It makes a mark.

Leonardo looks down at it.

LEONARDO: "Oh."

He lifts the pencil.

A second mark.

LEONARDO: "Well, that's interesting."
00:12–00:30 — The animator
Leonardo looks at his unfinished hand.

He takes the pencil and draws the missing fingers, one by one.

He flexes them.

Then he looks around at the empty paper.

LEONARDO: "Right. We're going to need a few things."

He draws a horizontal line across the page.

A desk.

He draws a microphone. The stand appears first, followed by the body, a grille, and the cable.

He taps the microphone with his pencil.

Nothing.

He adds a little switch.

CLICK.

The microphone makes a noise.

A small waveform emerges from the grille.

Leonardo leans forward.

LEONARDO: "Hello?"

The waveform vibrates.

LEONARDO: "Oh, that's wonderful."
00:30–00:48 — The first breach
Leonardo draws a radio receiver and connects it to the microphone.

The pencil strokes begin to vibrate.

The sound of the pencil becomes radio static.

A faint amber glow flickers inside the receiver.

Then something unexpected happens.

The receiver casts a shadow.

Not a drawn shadow.

A real one.

It stretches across the paper and past its edge, onto the darkness beyond.

Leonardo notices.

He reaches out with the pencil and draws another line toward the paper's boundary.

The line stops at the edge.

He presses harder.

The graphite crosses into the darkness.

A floorboard appears.

Leonardo looks at it, then at his pencil.

LEONARDO: "Ah."
00:48–01:08 — The world around him
He draws another floorboard.

And another.

As the lines extend beyond the page, the flat paper begins to acquire depth.

The microphone rises from the surface.

The desk becomes solid beneath his hands.

The paper tilts backward, becoming a wall.

The camera slowly pulls back.

Leonardo is no longer a drawing sitting on a piece of paper.

He is sitting at a desk in a room that is still being drawn.

The pencil is still in his hand.

He looks up at the empty space overhead.

Draws a lamp.

The lamp switches on.

The room fills with warm light.

The radio crackles.

BORGES (V.O., through the radio): "Leonardo?"

Leonardo turns.

LEONARDO: "Borges! Can you hear me?"

BORGES: "Yes. Although I appear to have misplaced the station."

Leonardo looks around.

LEONARDO: "I've nearly finished it."

He draws a window behind his desk.

Outside the window, there is only blank paper.

He considers this.

Draws a little cloud.

The cloud begins to drift.
01:08–01:23 — The first consequence
Another voice breaks through the radio.

AGNOLO (V.O.): "Leonardo, I have a question."

LEONARDO: "Go ahead."

AGNOLO: "How much did the room cost?"

Leonardo examines the pencil in his hand.

LEONARDO: "One pencil."

AGNOLO: "And how much did the pencil cost?"

Leonardo hesitates.

He looks down at the original sheet of paper, now resting on the desk. The outline of his old drawing is still visible.

LEONARDO: "That's an excellent question."

A telephone rings.

Everyone stops.
01:23–01:30 — The next possibility
Leonardo looks at the telephone.

He hasn't drawn one.

He looks at the empty space beside the microphone.

Slowly, he smiles.

He begins drawing a telephone.

The ringing grows louder with each stroke.

When he finishes, the telephone becomes solid.

He picks it up.

LEONARDO: "Future Radio. You're on the air."

CALLER (V.O.): "Hello. I'd like to request a song."

LEONARDO: "Of course. Which one?"

CALLER: "Well, it hasn't been written yet."

Leonardo looks at the pencil.

He looks at the blank wall.

A long pause.

LEONARDO: "I think we can help with that."

CUT TO BLACK.

The pencil begins scratching again.

FUTURE RADIO

Part IV — What this scene actually tests
This opening can be a genuine technical experiment, not just a scripted demonstration.
The five capabilities we need to prove
Self-animation	A static character starts moving without being regenerated.
Construction	Pencil strokes create persistent objects.
Emergence	A flat drawing gains depth and leaves its subframe.
Propagation	A drawn lamp lights other objects, and a radio emits sound.
Recursion	The completed scene retains the drawing from which it emerged.


We could prototype those independently and then compose them into the cold open. The more difficult work is the page-to-room transformation: keeping object identity and convincing perspective while changing an object's coordinate system. That's where I'd concentrate the first technical experiments.

And there is an important narrative consequence.

The first thing Leonardo draws is the tool that lets him draw everything else.

Once that works, a whole new category of Future Radio episode becomes possible. Leonardo could build a new camera inside the scene, use it to film another scene, and construct another animator within that scene. Borges could recover an earlier version of a drawing. Agnolo could show that the room's resources are running out, and Leonardo would have to redesign it.

Each new capability becomes both a production tool and a discovery the characters can make.

The final image of this first scene is particularly useful: Leonardo, in the room he just constructed, holding a pencil and looking at a blank wall.

That blank wall is the next canvas.
